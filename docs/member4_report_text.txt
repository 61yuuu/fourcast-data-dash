4. Validation
We evaluated both tracks using monthly walk-forward validation from December 2025 to May 2026, covering
the same 10,069 homes per track. Each month, models were fitted on transactions completed before that
month began and evaluated on sales within the month. Prediction inputs excluded sale prices, sale dates,
days on market, and third-party AVM fields. The off-market track additionally excluded asking prices. Category
encoders and other learned preprocessing were fitted within each training fold.

Historical features used different time cutoffs. Off-market historical sales summaries used only the fold’s
training transactions completed strictly before each home’s listing date. List-aware historical sale-to-list ratios
used the preceding three calendar months: training observations received summaries ending before their sale
month, while validation observations used a fixed training-history snapshot. The overall evaluation therefore
represents a monthly prediction setting, rather than a strict listing-day backtest.

Our evaluation pipeline checked missing values, duplicate records, positive finite prices, and consistency of
outcomes across model versions. We reported pooled median absolute percentage error (MdAPE), mean
absolute percentage error (MAPE), and median signed percentage error. Positive signed errors indicate
overprediction. We examined errors by property type, observed sale-price decile, and month, with additional
community analysis for both models. Models were selected by pooled MdAPE, with MAPE as the
tie-breaker.

The selected off-market model reduced MdAPE by 11.37 percentage points relative to its baseline. The

The selected models were history_text_boost for off-market and current_v3 for list-aware. In the latest

was retained. The list-aware model achieved lower errors, but the two tracks use different information sets; this
comparison does not isolate the effect of asking price alone.

6. Where the model fails




Figure 1. MdAPE (top) and median signed percentage error (bottom) by observed sale-price decile for the
selected models. Decile 1 contains the cheapest homes; blue denotes off-market and orange denotes
list-aware.

Price extremes remained challenging for the off-market model. In the most expensive decile, MdAPE was
14.27% and median signed error was −9.03%. In the cheapest decile, median signed error was approximately
+10.8%. This tendency to overpredict cheaper homes and underpredict expensive homes persisted despite
the near-zero overall median signed error.

The list-aware model had smaller directional errors, from approximately +2.3% in the cheapest decile to −0.5%
in the highest deciles. Its MdAPE was nevertheless higher in the two highest deciles, at approximately 5.5%,
compared with 3.6%–3.9% in the lower five deciles. Sale-price groups were used only for diagnosis, never as
predictors.

Figure 2. MdAPE (top) and median signed percentage error (bottom) by property type for the selected models.
Blue denotes off-market and orange denotes list-aware.

Off-market MdAPE was highest for co-op apartments (14.59%), although this group contained only 48 homes.
Among the other property types, detached homes had the highest MdAPE (10.35%). List-aware MdAPE
ranged from 3.55% for condo apartments to 5.81% for freehold townhouses. Differences may reflect property
characteristics, subgroup composition, and available training examples; these comparisons do not establish
their causes.

Monthly MdAPE ranged from approximately 9.1% to 9.9% for off-market and 4.0% to 4.7% for list-aware.
Off-market monthly median signed errors ranged from approximately −1.9% to +3.9%, showing directional
variation concealed by the overall summary.

Community-level errors varied across both models. Of 145 communities, 132 had at least 30 validation homes
and were included in the ranking. The highest community MdAPE was 17.50% in Lindenglen for the off-market
model (n = 62) and 9.22% in Sycamorehaven for the list-aware model (n = 31). These subgroup estimates
should be interpreted cautiously, particularly for communities with small samples.




7. Limitations
The same six months were repeatedly used for model development and selection. Reported scores are
therefore development-validation results, not performance on an untouched test set, and may be optimistic.

Monthly training cutoffs can include transactions completed after a validation home was listed. Although the
off-market historical summaries separately enforce listing-date cutoffs, this does not make the entire fitted
model a listing-day forecast. Strict listing-day evaluation would require a separate backtest.

Subgroup results are descriptive and lack confidence intervals in the current evaluation pipeline. Small
samples and shared monthly market effects limit their interpretation. Median errors also conceal large
individual misses: approximately 17.75% of off-market predictions exceeded 20% absolute percentage error,
compared with 1.32% for list-aware predictions.

Finally, the data and community locations are synthetic. These results do not demonstrate accuracy on real
Toronto transactions. Future work should prioritize stricter prediction-time evaluation, uncertainty estimates,
and investigation of expensive homes and other poorly predicted subgroups.
